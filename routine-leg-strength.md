# 4-Day Leg-Strength + Cardio Program — v1

## Lifter profile
- **Experience:** Beginner (< ~1 year consistent training)
- **Primary goal:** **Leg strength with a glute emphasis**, plus regular cardio. Arms are *not* a priority (upper body kept to light maintenance only).
- **Schedule:** Train Mon / Tue / Thu / Fri. Rest Wed / Sat / Sun. (Same days as her partner.)
- **Session cap:** ≤ 1 hr 15 min door-to-door (≈ 10 min warm-up + ~60 min work).
- **Available equipment:** Barbell + power rack, adjustable bench, full dumbbell set, kettlebells, cable/pulley station, lat pulldown + seated row, leg machines (press / curl / extension), pull-up + dip station, trap bar if available (otherwise conventional/RDL).

## The recovery problem — and how this solves it
Training legs **3×/week** looks like it can't recover, but that's only true if all three days are the *same* heavy stimulus. This program uses **daily undulating periodization (DUP)** — each leg day rotates pattern and intensity:

- **Mon — Squat / quad-dominant, heavy (low reps).**
- **Thu — Hinge / posterior-dominant, heavy (low reps).** Different pattern, so Monday's quads are recovering while the posterior chain works.
- **Fri — Light, higher-rep, unilateral + machines.** No grinding — quality and pump only.

Mapped onto Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri:
- The two genuinely **heavy** days (Mon, Thu) are **2 rest-days apart** — full recovery.
- **Tuesday cardio** sits between them as active recovery.
- The only back-to-back (**Thu→Fri**) is fine because Friday is the deliberately *light* day, and it's followed by Sat/Sun rest.
- **HIIT lives on Friday** (before the weekend), so hard conditioning never robs a heavy lifting day.

That's how you get the third leg session in safely.

## Guiding principles
- **Strength first.** The two heavy days are built around a main barbell lift in a true strength rep range (5s), loaded fresh and first.
- **Effort (RIR):** Stop ~**2–3 reps short of failure** while learning technique. You build strength through quality reps, not by grinding to failure.
- **Progression (beginner linear progression):** On the main lifts, add the smallest increment (~2.5 kg) **each week** as long as all prescribed reps are clean at the target RIR. On accessories, use **double progression** (hit the top of the rep range on all sets → add load). This simple, fast-moving progression is exactly what a beginner should use before needing anything fancier.
- **Glute emphasis:** Hip thrusts and glute-biased unilateral work feature on every leg day; the heavy squat and deadlift drive overall lower-body strength and load the glutes hard as well.
- **Tempo / ROM:** Controlled lowering (~2 sec), full depth/range on every rep. Technique before load.
- **Logbook:** Record load, sets, reps and RIR every session. The plan is to add weight over time — track it.

## Mesocycle progression (beginner linear progression → deload)
A beginner can add load almost every week — use it while it lasts.

| Weeks | Main lifts (squat / deadlift) | Effort | Accessories |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** (intro) | Establish a working 4×5 at **3 RIR**; perfect technique | 3 RIR | Learn the movements, 2–3 RIR |
| **3–5** (build) | **+2.5 kg/week** while all 5s stay clean | 2–3 RIR | Double progression; add a set to hip thrust if recovering well |
| **6** (deload) | −10–20% load, 2×5 easy | 4–5 RIR | Half the sets |
| **7+** | Resume heavier; when weekly jumps stall on a lift, switch that lift to adding reps before load, then re-deload | 2 RIR | Continue double progression |

When linear weekly jumps stop working (usually a couple of months in), that's the cue to move the main lifts onto the **DUP intensities the layout already supports** (heavier/lower-rep on one day, moderate on another). Take a deload sooner if sleep, joints, or bar speed decline.

## Recovery & lifestyle (part of the program)
- **Protein:** ~**1.6–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day**, across 3–4 meals — the substrate for building strength and glutes.
- **Sleep:** **7–9 hours/night** — the main recovery and strength-adaptation signal.
- **Calories:** eat to support training; a small surplus aids strength/muscle gain, maintenance if body-composition is the aim. Hydrate well.
- **Managing the leg load:** if quads/glutes are still very sore on a heavy day, reduce that day's top load ~10% rather than skipping — frequency drives a beginner's progress.

## Cardio / conditioning plan
She enjoys variety, so it's built in across the week without compromising leg recovery:
- **Tue — Zone 2 steady, 25–35 min** (easy run, incline-walk, bike or rower; conversational pace). Placed between the heavy days as *active recovery* — keep it genuinely easy so it doesn't dent Thursday.
- **Fri — HIIT finisher, ~10–14 min** (e.g. 6–8 × 30 s hard / 90 s easy on bike or rower, or treadmill intervals). Hard conditioning sits here, before two rest days.
- **Optional — weekend Zone 2, 20–30 min** (Sat or Sun) if she wants more easy mileage.
- Keep Tuesday and weekend work in Zone 2; save the hard intervals for Friday.

## Warm-up (every lifting session, ~10 min)
1. 3–4 min easy cardio (bike/row) to raise core temp.
2. **Glute activation:** banded glute bridges + lateral band walks, 1–2 sets.
3. Dynamic hip/ankle mobility.
4. 2–3 ramp-up sets on the day's main lift (empty bar → ~60% → working weight).

## Why this works for her
- **Legs 3×/week, safely.** DUP rotates squat-heavy → hinge-heavy → light, so frequency is high but no pattern is maxed twice in a row. The two heavy days are spaced 2 rest-days apart; the only back-to-back is the deliberately light Friday.
- **Strength-led.** Heavy 5-rep squats and deadlifts loaded fresh and first, with simple beginner linear progression — the fastest route to leg strength for a novice.
- **Glutes prioritised.** 15 direct glute sets/week plus heavy indirect work; hip thrusts and glute-biased unilateral movements on every leg day.
- **Cardio she enjoys, sequenced smartly.** Zone 2 as active recovery (Tue), HIIT before the weekend (Fri), optional easy weekend mileage — variety without hurting leg recovery.
- **No wasted effort.** Upper body kept to light maintenance only, matching her "arms aren't a priority" brief and keeping every session inside the 75-minute cap.
