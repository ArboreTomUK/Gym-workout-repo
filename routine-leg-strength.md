# 4-Day Leg-Strength + Cardio Program — v2

## Lifter profile
- **Experience:** Beginner (< ~1 year consistent training)
- **Primary goal:** **Leg strength with a glute emphasis**, plus regular cardio. Arms are *not* a priority (upper body kept to light maintenance only).
- **Schedule:** Train Mon / Tue / Thu / Fri. Rest Wed / Sat / Sun. (Same days as her partner.)
- **Session cap:** ≤ 1 hr 15 min door-to-door (≈ 10 min warm-up + ~60 min work).
- **Available equipment:** Barbell + power rack, adjustable bench, full dumbbell set, **kettlebells**, cable/pulley station, lat pulldown + seated row, leg machines (press / curl / extension), pull-up + dip station, trap bar if available (otherwise conventional/RDL).

## The recovery problem — and how this solves it
Training legs **3×/week** looks like it can't recover, but that's only true if all three days are the *same* heavy stimulus. This program uses **daily undulating periodization (DUP)** — each leg day rotates pattern and intensity:

- **Mon — Squat / quad-dominant, heavy (low reps).**
- **Thu — Hinge / posterior-dominant, heavy (low reps).** Different pattern, so Monday's quads are recovering while the posterior chain works.
- **Fri — Light, explosive + higher-rep, unilateral + machines.** No grinding — power, quality and pump only.

Mapped onto Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri:
- The two genuinely **heavy** days (Mon, Thu) are **2 rest-days apart** — full recovery.
- **Tuesday cardio** sits between them as active recovery.
- The only back-to-back (**Thu→Fri**) is fine because Friday is the deliberately *light* day, and it's followed by Sat/Sun rest. **If Thursday's soreness lingers, trim Friday's hip-thrust/curl volume first** — that's the built-in release valve.
- **Hard conditioning lives on Friday** (before the weekend), so it never robs a heavy lift.

That's how you get the third leg session in safely.

## Guiding principles
- **Strength first.** The two heavy days are built around a main barbell lift in a true strength rep range (5s), loaded fresh and first.
- **Effort (RIR):** Stop ~**2–3 reps short of failure** while learning technique; on accessories in the build weeks you can push to ~1–2 RIR. You build strength through quality reps, not by grinding to failure.
- **Power as a skill (kettlebell swing):** The swing is trained explosively, **fresh**, with a crisp full hip snap — **stop a set the moment the snap loses power.** It builds glute/hip-hinge force production and doubles as conditioning that reinforces the pattern she's training.
- **Progression (beginner linear progression):** On the main lifts, add the smallest increment (~2.5 kg) **each week** as long as all prescribed reps are clean at the target RIR. On accessories, use **double progression**. On the swing, add load (heavier bell) once 5×15 stays crisp.
- **Glute emphasis:** Hip thrusts and glute-biased work feature on every leg day; the heavy squat and deadlift drive overall lower-body strength and load the glutes hard as well.
- **Tempo / ROM:** Controlled lowering (~2 sec) on grinds, full depth/range every rep. Technique before load.
- **Logbook:** Record load, sets, reps and RIR every session. Re-test a working 5-rep load (or a rep PR) on the main lifts every ~6 weeks as a performance checkpoint.
- **Permission to trim:** If a session is running past the cap, cut the last calf/core/leg-extension set first — never the main lift.

## Weekly direct-set volume (counted from the sessions below)
Counting rule: hip thrust, glute-focused back extension, and Bulgarian split squat are credited to **glutes**; lunges to **quads**; KB swings are listed separately as explosive **power/conditioning** (a big glute/posterior stimulus on top, not counted as hypertrophy sets).

| Muscle / quality | Priority | Direct sets/week | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glutes** | #1 | **15** | Mon hip thrust 3 · Thu hip thrust 3 + split squat 3 + back ext 3 · Fri hip thrust 3 |
| **Quads** | #2 (legs) | **12** | Mon squat 4 + walking lunge 3 · Fri goblet/leg press 3 + leg ext 2 |
| **Hamstrings** | #2 (legs) | **9** | Mon leg curl 3 · Thu deadlift 4 · Fri leg curl 2 |
| **KB swing (power/conditioning)** | — | 5 | Fri (explosive, fresh) — extra glute/posterior stimulus |
| **Calves** | — | 9 | Mon 3 · Thu 3 · Fri 3 |
| **Abs / core** | — | 6 | Mon 3 · Thu 3 |
| **Cardio** | tied #2 | 2–3 sessions | Tue Zone 2 · Fri swing/HIIT · (opt. weekend Zone 2) |
| Back (maintenance) | — | 6 | Tue pulldown 3 + seated row 3 |
| Chest (maintenance) | — | 3 | Tue DB press / push-up 3 |
| Rear delts (posture) | — | 3 | Tue face pull 3 |

**Glutes lead the program** at 15 direct sets (top of the beginner-appropriate range) *plus* heavy indirect loading from every squat, deadlift, lunge, and the explosive swings. Quads and hamstrings are well-served by the main lifts. Upper body is intentionally minimal — just enough to stay balanced and protect posture.

## Mesocycle progression (beginner linear progression → deload)
| Weeks | Main lifts (squat / deadlift) | Effort | Accessories / swing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** (intro) | Establish a working 4×5 at **3 RIR**; perfect technique | 3 RIR | Learn the movements; swing light & crisp |
| **3–5** (build) | **+2.5 kg/week** while all 5s stay clean | 2–3 RIR | Double progression; push accessories to ~1–2 RIR; add a set to hip thrust if recovering well |
| **6** (deload) | −10–20% load, 2×5 easy | 4–5 RIR | Half the sets; light swings only |
| **7+** | Resume heavier; when weekly jumps stall on a lift, switch that lift to adding reps before load, then re-deload | 2 RIR | Continue double progression |

When linear weekly jumps stop working (usually a couple of months in), that's the cue to move the main lifts onto the **DUP intensities the layout already supports** (heavier/lower-rep on one day, moderate on another). Take a deload sooner if sleep, joints, or bar speed decline.

**Where to add volume first (build weeks):** if she's recovering well, the first extra set goes to the hip thrust (cap the in-mesocycle glute build at ~+2 sets before the deload). The next is a **third weekly leg-curl exposure** — hamstrings are the leanest leg muscle here (much of their 9 sets comes from the deadlift, where they work at short length), so an added direct curl is the highest-value addition after glutes.

## Recovery & lifestyle (part of the program)
- **Protein:** ~**1.6–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day**, across 3–4 meals — the substrate for building strength and glutes.
- **Sleep:** **7–9 hours/night** — the main recovery and strength-adaptation signal.
- **Calories:** eat to support training; a small surplus aids strength/muscle gain, maintenance if body-composition is the aim. Hydrate well.
- **Managing the leg load:** if quads/glutes are still very sore on a heavy day, reduce that day's top load ~10% rather than skipping — frequency drives a beginner's progress. Friday posterior volume is the first thing to cut if Thursday DOMS carries over.

## Cardio / conditioning plan
Built in across the week without compromising leg recovery:
- **Tue — Zone 2 steady, 25–30 min** (easy run, incline-walk, bike or rower; conversational pace). Active recovery between the heavy days — keep it genuinely easy so it doesn't dent Thursday.
- **Fri — Kettlebell-swing conditioning** (the 5 explosive sets below) — ballistic intervals that double as her hard conditioning. Add **8–10 min of treadmill/bike intervals** afterward if she wants more.
- **Optional — weekend Zone 2, 20–30 min** (Sat or Sun) for extra easy mileage.
- Keep Tuesday and weekend work in Zone 2; save the hard, explosive work for Friday.

## Warm-up (every lifting session, ~10 min)
1. 3–4 min easy cardio (bike/row) to raise core temp.
2. **Glute activation:** banded glute bridges + lateral band walks, 1–2 sets.
3. Dynamic hip/ankle mobility.
4. 2–3 ramp-up sets on the day's main lift (empty bar → ~60% → working weight).

## Why this works for her
- **Legs 3×/week, safely.** DUP rotates squat-heavy → hinge-heavy → light/explosive, so frequency is high but no pattern is maxed twice in a row. The two heavy days are spaced 2 rest-days apart; the only back-to-back is the deliberately light Friday, with a built-in volume release valve.
- **Strength-led.** Heavy 5-rep squats and deadlifts loaded fresh and first, with simple beginner linear progression — the fastest route to leg strength for a novice.
- **Glutes prioritised, with real hip power.** 15 direct glute sets/week plus heavy indirect work, and the kettlebell swing — the gym's best hinge-and-glute power tool — trained explosively as a skill.
- **Cardio she enjoys, sequenced smartly.** Zone 2 as active recovery (Tue), explosive swing/HIIT conditioning before the weekend (Fri), optional easy weekend mileage — variety without hurting leg recovery.
- **No wasted effort.** Upper body kept to light maintenance only, matching her brief and keeping every session inside the 75-minute cap.
